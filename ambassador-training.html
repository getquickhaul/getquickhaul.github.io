Ambassador Training Course

Short, practical, and everything you need in your head before you talk to anyone. Six lessons. Read them once, keep this page on your phone, and you'll always know what to do.

Lesson 1 — Your look

You are the brand before the van ever shows up. Dress clean and neat — clean shirt, tidy shoes, a smile. Not a suit, not a sales costume: like a good neighbor who represents something real. When your kit arrives (shirt, cards, flyers with your code), wear and carry it. People trust what looks established.

Lesson 2 — Where to go

Go where your neighborhood already gathers:

· Your own building and block — you already know these people.
· Small stores: bodegas, bakeries, furniture and appliance stores, mattress shops — their customers always need deliveries.
· Barbershops, salons, laundromats — people wait there, and waiting people listen.
· Churches and community boards, neighborhood events, flea markets.
· Storage facilities and realtors — everybody there is moving something.

Start with the places that know your face. Ten real conversations beat a hundred strangers.

Lesson 3 — How to approach a person

One person at a time, friendly, twenty seconds. Never open with a pitch — open with yourself:

"Hi, I'm [your name] — I'm a Community Ambassador for GetQuickHaul, our neighborhood pickup and delivery platform. If you ever need something big picked up or delivered — furniture, a store purchase, boxes — our drivers are neighbors with vans, from $45. Here's my card — my code is on it, use it when you book."

Then ask about them: Are you moving soon? Does your family buy big things often? Listen more than you talk. If they're not interested, thank them warmly and move on — a good no today is a yes next month.

Lesson 4 — What to say in a store

Ask for the owner or the manager, and keep it to thirty seconds:

"Good morning — I'm [your name], Community Ambassador for GetQuickHaul. We do pickups and deliveries for local stores — when your customer buys something big, we can deliver it the same day, from $45. Stores also open business accounts with weekly routes and one weekly invoice. Can I leave a few cards at the counter, and a flyer for the window? My code is on them."

Always ask permission before leaving anything. Thank them either way. Then tell the office which store you visited — call or text (551) 233-8583 — so we can follow up like professionals.

Lesson 5 — Getting the app downloaded

A download is worth more than a flyer, because the app stays on their phone. Do it with them, not at them:

1. Show your own phone with the app open — let them see it's real.
2. Send them the link or open it together: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6823931211
3. Stay while it downloads. Find the icon on their home screen before you walk away.
4. Show them how a booking starts — one address, a couple of taps.
5. Remind them: "Use my code when you book." That code is how your 5% finds you.

Lesson 6 — What never to promise

· Never an exact price for a trip — their booking gives them their exact price.
· Never a pickup time the office hasn't confirmed.
· Never how much a driver will earn — drivers build their own business; the platform brings the trips.
· When you don't know, say so, and send them to the office: (551) 233-8583. Honesty is the whole brand.
